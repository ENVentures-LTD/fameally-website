How to Share a Shopping List Between iPhone and Android

Connect the same household list, check both phones and make the next shared shop easier.

By Edward Nickless · Fameally Published 4 November 2026 8 minute read

One person has an iPhone, the other has an Android phone, and both buy things for the same home. A shopping list should make that easier. It shouldn't mean sending a fresh screenshot every time somebody remembers the milk.

To share a shopping list between iPhone and Android, choose an app available on both, give each person access to the same list or household, then check that changes travel in both directions. Installing the same app is the starting point; the shared list still needs to be connected.

This guide walks through that setup using Fameally, with Google Keep as a simpler shared-checklist option. Start with two people and a few ordinary items before moving the whole weekly shop.

Choose a shared list that works on both phones

Begin with what you want to share. If the job is simply “we both need to add groceries and tick them off”, a collaborative checklist may be enough. If one person plans dinners and another shops, keeping meals, plans and ingredients together may be more useful.

  • Fameally: available on iPhone, iPad and Android, with shared meals, plans and shopping lists through the Family plan.
  • Google Keep: its iPhone and Android apps let you collaborate on notes and lists. That gives you a straightforward place to maintain a checklist together.

For a wider shortlist, see our comparison of shopping-list apps for households. Check sharing access as well as download availability: an app can be available on both phones while keeping collaboration in a particular plan.

A sent list and a shared list do different jobs. A screenshot or copied message records what was needed at that moment. A collaborative list lets the other person see later edits in the same place. Choose collaboration when both people will keep making changes.

Check accounts and sharing access

Install the app from the official store on each phone. For Fameally, use the App Store listing on iPhone and the Google Play listing on Android. Check the listing for your device's current compatibility requirements.

Each person should use their own account. Sharing a household doesn't require sharing a password, an Apple Account or a Google account. Make a note of the email address used inside the shopping app, especially if it differs from the address used for other things on that phone.

Before sending an invitation

  • Agree which household will hold the shared shopping list.
  • Check the app-account email address of the person being invited.
  • Confirm that the household has the sharing access it needs.
  • Connect both phones to the internet for setup and the first check.

Fameally's published plans place household sharing in Family, where the owner can invite up to five other people by email. Check current Family access and terms before buying a plan. An app-store purchase and an invitation to join a Fameally household are separate parts of the setup.

Connect one Fameally household

Choose the person whose household you want to use as the owner for this setup. Then follow the family invitation process in Fameally:

  1. The owner sends the invitation. Use the other person's Fameally account email address, checked together rather than guessed from an old contact.
  2. The other person signs in with that address. Open Fameally on the second phone and confirm the account before looking for the invitation.
  3. Accept the invitation in the app. Sending it is only part of joining. Read any access or workspace information shown before confirming.
  4. Check the shared family workspace. Both people should be looking at the household they agreed to use, then at the same shopping list.

If either person already has meals or plans elsewhere, take time to understand the join prompts. Don't assume that two existing collections will combine in a particular way. Ask Fameally support if a prompt doesn't make the outcome clear.

Run the two-phone milk check

Before relying on the list in a supermarket, sit together with both phones online. Choose an item you genuinely need, or use a clearly labelled test item and remove it afterwards. The point is to check your own setup in both directions.

A simple check to repeat on your phones

  1. Add from iPhone. One person adds “milk — one carton” to the shared shopping list.
  2. Read on Android. The other person finds that exact item in the same household list and checks the quantity.
  3. Change from Android. If the household needs two cartons, update the quantity using the list's editing controls.
  4. Read back on iPhone. Confirm the revised quantity is visible before either person continues.
  5. Check completion. Use an item already bought, or a test item, to confirm that both people understand how a completed purchase is shown.

If milk isn't really being bought, don't leave the example marked as a real purchase. Remove the test item or return the genuine item to its correct state. A setup check shouldn't make the shopping history misleading.

Allow the app to finish loading, and check the result rather than assuming a particular update speed. This is a practical verification routine for your phones, not a claim that every change arrives within a fixed number of seconds.

Avoid using the first shopping trip to find out how disconnected phones behave. Confirm important changes when both devices are online. If the two views still disagree, agree which visible list the shopper will follow and contact support instead of buying from conflicting copies.

For a useful support request, include both device models, app versions, the account involved and the change that failed to appear. Keep passwords and verification codes out of the message. Don't delete accounts or household content as an improvised synchronisation fix.

Use Google Keep for a shared checklist

If you only need a collaborative note, Google documents a sharing route on both iPhone and Android. Create one shopping checklist in Keep, then open it and add the other person as a collaborator.

  • On Android: open the note, choose Action, then Collaborator, select the other person's name or email and save.
  • On iPhone: open the note, choose Action, then Collaborators, select the person and finish with Done.

Google's Android sharing instructions and iPhone sharing instructions explain the current controls. Adding a collaborator lets that person edit the shared note; sending a copy through another app is a separate action.

Repeat the milk check in Keep too. Google also notes that deleting a shared note you own deletes it for everyone, so keep the ongoing list rather than treating it as a disposable message.

Before the first shared shop

  • Both people can open the agreed list on their own phone.
  • An addition from each phone is visible on the other.
  • Quantities and completed items make sense to both shoppers.
  • Everyone knows which list to use for the next trip.

A few common questions

Do we need matching phones?
No. Use a service available on both platforms, then connect the accounts to the same shared list or household.
Do we need to use the same login?
For Fameally's family invitation route, each person uses their own account and joins through the invited email address. That keeps household access separate from sharing a password.
Does sending a screenshot keep a list shared?
A screenshot can communicate a snapshot. It doesn't let the recipient see later changes to the original list or update it themselves.
Is the two-phone example a synchronisation test result?
It's a setup check for you to carry out on your own phones. No update-speed or offline-reliability result is claimed here.

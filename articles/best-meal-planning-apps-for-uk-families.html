Best meal planning apps for UK families in 2026

A practical buyer guide for choosing the app that fits your actual household routine: family meals, reusable weeks, shopping lists, recipes, and the weekly food shop.

By Edward Nickless Published Saturday 18th July 2026

The best meal planning app for a UK family is not always the one with the biggest recipe library or the longest feature list. It is the one that helps your household get through a normal week: deciding meals, checking what needs buying, sharing the plan, reducing forgotten items, and making the food shop less last-minute. This guide compares popular meal planning apps by the job they do best, so you can choose the tool that fits your family rather than the one that simply looks busiest.

Quick answer: which app should you choose?

If you want a UK-first family workflow from meal plan to shopping list, start with Fameally. If you mainly want trusted recipe inspiration, Good Food is a strong UK option. If you want recipe-led healthy dinners, look at Mealime. If you already collect recipes from everywhere, Paprika or Plan to Eat may suit you. If the shared grocery list matters more than the meal plan, AnyList is hard to ignore. If you want a broad recipe discovery app, Samsung Food is worth checking. If you need a shared family calendar as much as meal planning, Cozi may fit better.

That is not a neat one-size-fits-all answer, but family food planning rarely behaves neatly. The right app depends on where your week breaks: ideas, recipes, planning, shopping, sharing, or remembering the regular household items that do not belong to any recipe.

How this list was chosen

This guide is written for UK households rather than generic app-store browsing. The apps included here either have strong meal planning, grocery list, recipe library, or family-sharing features. They are not all trying to solve the same problem, which is exactly why comparing them by use case is more useful than pretending there is a single winner for every family.

Fameally is designed around the route many families actually need: save the meals your household eats, add them to the week, generate the food shopping list, and keep regular household items organised too. Good Food: best for trusted UK recipe inspiration

Good Food is a familiar name in UK kitchens, and its app is strongest when you want trusted recipe inspiration, app-only recipes, organised favourites, cook mode, and meal plans. For many families, that kind of reliable recipe library is still the starting point for planning a week.

The official Good Food app page describes meal plans for saving time and money, recipe organisation, instant dinner inspiration, and cook mode. Its FAQ also describes meal plans with pre-made shopping lists. That makes it a good choice if your family mainly needs good recipe ideas and planned menus from a known UK food brand.

The trade-off is focus. Good Food is primarily a recipe and food content app. If your main need is a shared household planning workflow with regular items, generated food lists, and family workspace features, you may want something more routine-led alongside or instead of it.

Mealime: best for recipe-led healthy weeknight meals

Mealime is a strong option for families who want help choosing what to cook, especially if healthy weeknight recipes and personal preferences matter. Its official site positions the app around planning, shopping, and cooking, with personalisation options and an organised grocery list generated from the weekly meal plan.

This works well when the household is happy to cook from the app's recipe suggestions. If the hardest part of your week is deciding what to eat and you want the app to guide you toward dinners, Mealime can be genuinely useful.

It may be less ideal if your family already has a settled set of meals and the job is mostly to reuse them, coordinate the shop, and remember household items. In that case, a routine-first planner may fit better.

Where Fameally fits among these apps

Some apps in this list begin with recipes. Fameally begins with the family routine: what are we eating, what do we need to buy, and who needs to see the plan? That is why it suits households that already have familiar meals but want the week and the shop to feel calmer.

A useful week can also become a starting point for the next one. Instead of starting from a blank page, you can keep the meals and structures that worked, then swap what needs changing.

Paprika: best for recipe collectors who want control

Paprika is one of the strongest choices for people who collect recipes from websites, family notes, cookbooks, and old favourites. Official Paprika materials describe recipe importing, cloud sync, smart grocery lists, a pantry, weekly or monthly meal planning, and reusable menus.

For a family that already knows what it likes and wants a serious recipe manager, Paprika can be excellent. It is also good for cooks who care about recipe scaling, offline access, pantry tracking, and a more controlled recipe library.

The trade-off is that Paprika asks you to manage the recipe system. It is powerful, but that power is most useful if someone in the household enjoys keeping the recipe library tidy. The official site also notes that each platform is sold separately, so check the cost on the devices your family uses.

Plan to Eat: best for serious weekly planners

Plan to Eat is built for people who like planning from their own recipes. Its official site describes recipe saving, recipe organisation, a meal planning calendar, and an automated shopping list that populates from planned recipes. Its help docs also mention staples and date-range shopping lists.

That makes it a good fit for families with a growing recipe library and a habit of sitting down to plan the week. It can be more structured than a notes app, and it is particularly useful if your issue is moving from recipes to a clear shop.

Plan to Eat's official pricing pages list prices in US dollars, and UK app-store pricing may vary. It is worth checking the current store listing before starting, especially if you are comparing annual costs.

AnyList: best if the shared shopping list is the main problem

AnyList is often loved because it does shared grocery lists very well. Its official pages describe list sharing, automatic item grouping, recipe organisation, Siri entry, meal planning calendars, and grocery lists generated from meal plans in AnyList Complete.

For families where the shop is the main pain point, AnyList is a strong contender. It is especially useful when multiple people add items and need the list to stay synced. If the household already has dinner planning under control but keeps forgetting items, a shopping-list-first app can make sense.

The trade-off is that the deeper meal planning features sit behind AnyList Complete. If your main need is a family meal planning workflow, not just a shared grocery list, compare carefully before committing.

Samsung Food: best for broad recipe discovery and free planning

Samsung Food is a broad recipe, cooking, meal planning, and grocery app. Its official site describes recipe saving, recipe discovery, weekly meal plans, one-click smart shopping lists, recipe sharing, and nutrition features. Samsung's help pages also describe turning a meal plan into a shopping list and sharing meal plans.

This makes Samsung Food a good option if you want a large recipe ecosystem and a free app to explore. It may suit families that enjoy discovering new dinners, saving recipes from around the web, and trying different meal ideas.

The trade-off is breadth. Because Samsung Food does so much, some households may find it more than they need if the goal is simply to reuse familiar meals and get the weekly shop done.

Cozi: best if family calendar organisation matters too

Cozi is wider than meal planning. Its official pages describe a shared family calendar, shopping lists, to-dos, recipes, and meal planning tools. Cozi's plan comparison also lists AI-powered recipe and meal planning features in Cozi Max.

That makes Cozi worth considering if food planning is only one part of the family admin problem. If your household also needs a shared calendar, to-do lists, and general coordination, Cozi may reduce the number of separate tools.

The trade-off is depth. If meal planning and the food shop are the main jobs, a dedicated meal planning and shopping list app may feel more direct.

Which meal planning app is best for your family?

Choose by the job, not by the biggest feature list. If your family is tired of rewriting the same meals and shopping lists, pick an app that reuses weeks and turns ingredients into a list. If your issue is dinner inspiration, choose a recipe-led app. If your recipes are scattered everywhere, choose a recipe manager. If the shopping list is the real source of chaos, choose the best shared list.

For many UK families, the everyday workflow matters more than recipe discovery. The question is not "Which app has the most dinner ideas?" It is "Which app will still be open next Thursday when someone asks what is for dinner and whether we need milk?"

Use the one-week test

Before paying annually for anything, run one normal week through the app.

  • Add meals your household already eats, not just new recipes.
  • Plan the week around real commitments, clubs, work, and tired evenings.
  • Turn the plan into a shopping list and check what is already at home.
  • Add household extras like cleaning, toiletries, bread, milk, cereal, and packed-lunch items.
  • Share the plan or list with the person who needs to see it.
  • At the end, ask whether next week will be quicker because this week now exists.

FAQ

What is the best meal planning app for UK families?
The best choice depends on your routine. Fameally is a strong fit for UK families who want reusable meal plans and shopping lists together. Good Food is strong for UK recipe inspiration, while AnyList is strong for shared grocery lists.
Which meal planning app is best for shopping lists?
Fameally, AnyList, Plan to Eat, Paprika, Mealime, and Samsung Food all connect meal planning with shopping lists in different ways. Choose based on whether you start from family meals, recipes, or the shared grocery list.
Is a recipe app the same as a meal planning app?
Not always. A recipe app helps you find or organise recipes. A meal planning app should also help place meals into the week and turn them into a practical shopping list.
Should families use a free meal planning app?
A free app can be enough if you mostly need ideas or a simple list. A paid app may be worth it when it saves repeated work, supports sharing, reuses meal plans, or reduces missed shopping items.
What should I look for in a family meal planning app?
Look for reusable meals, a weekly view, shopping lists from ingredients, shared access, household list categories, and enough flexibility to handle changed plans.

Invite your household and share meals, plans and shopping lists in Fameally

Get the owner and invited person into the same household workspace, then check the shared list together.

By Edward Nickless · FameallyPublished 21 November 2026

When more than one person cooks or shops, start by getting everyone into the same household workspace. That gives you a common place to find the meals, plan and shopping list.

This guide takes the owner and the invited person through the same setup, then gives you a small shared-list check to do together. Have both devices available so you can resolve the account details before relying on the list during a shop.

In this tutorial

  1. Check access and accounts
  2. Owner: invite the intended person
  3. Invitee: accept with the right account
  4. Check the workspace together
  5. Resolve missing invitations or content
  6. Use the shared plan for the next shop

Check access and accounts

Household sharing needs Family access. Fameally's published plan information allows the owner to invite up to five other people, making six people including the owner. Check current plans and access if sharing isn't available.

The owner screens were checked and captured on Fameally 1.9.77, Android 14, using a Pixel 8 emulator on 21 November 2026. Menus and layout can differ in another version or on iPhone and iPad.

  • For the owner: sign in to the account that owns the family workspace and check that Family access is available.
  • For the invited person: agree which email address they will use for their own Fameally account.
  • For both: have an internet connection, and check which account is currently signed in.

Fameally supports shared meals, plans and lists across iPhone, iPad and Android. These screenshots show Android.

A good first example is the two people who will use the next shopping list. Finish their setup and shared check before inviting the rest of the household.

Owner: invite the intended person

  1. Confirm the address together. Ask the person which address they use, or intend to use, to sign in to Fameally. Check spelling before sending.
  2. Open User → Account → Manage Family. This opens Family Management. In Family summary, check your role, member count, shared data source and subscription status. The checked demonstration account showed Role: Owner, three of six members and Subscription: Active.
  3. Find Invite member. Enter the intended address and check it once more before choosing Send invite. The screen explains that a Fameally user can belong to only one family at a time.
  4. Ask the person to continue on their device. Sending the invitation and joining the workspace are separate parts of the setup.

Use an address the person controls. They should sign in to their own account rather than receive the owner's password.

Invitee: accept with the right account

  1. Open Fameally on your device. Sign in with the address the owner invited.
  2. Find the invitation in the app. Check that it is from the household you intend to join.
  3. Read the joining information. Review any information the app presents about your access or workspace before confirming. If the effect on your existing setup is unclear, pause and ask support.
  4. Accept the invitation. Then check the shared content with the owner before using it for a real shop.

Keep the account check simple: read the address in your account and compare it with the address the owner invited. Two similar-looking addresses can still be different accounts.

Check the workspace together

Choose a quiet moment before the shop. First compare one meal or plan you expect to share. Check its name and the intended week, so that looking at different dates doesn't resemble missing content.

Then try a small list change that the household agrees to make. For example, if milk really needs buying, add it once and ask the other person to look for it in the same shopping list. Check the item and quantity together before either person edits it again.

If you use demonstration data instead, agree on an obvious test entry and clean it up together afterwards. Avoid experimenting with an important meal plan while somebody is already following it in the supermarket.

Both people should be able to identify the shared content they intend to use. If they can't, use the checks below before continuing. Keep both devices connected while checking the list; updates can depend on connectivity.

Resolve missing invitations or content

Missing email appears
The checked Android build shows Missing email and Enter an email address to invite. when Send invite is selected with the field empty. Choose OK, enter the intended person's address and check it before continuing.
The owner can't invite someone
Check that the owner is signed in to the intended account, has Family access and is within the published five-invitee limit. If access appears wrong, contact support before changing or buying another plan.
The invited person can't find the invitation
Compare the invited address with the address used to sign in. Check the invitation in the app; searching an email inbox alone doesn't establish whether the app invitation is available. If the addresses match and it is still missing, contact support.
The invitation was accepted, but content is missing
Check that both people are in the same family workspace and connected to the internet. Compare the intended plan or list together. Don't create a second copy of the whole week while you're still working out which workspace each person can see.
A list change hasn't appeared
Confirm the account, workspace and list first. Make one agreed change and check it on the other device. If the result remains unclear, stop using test changes and ask support to help investigate.

For help, use Contact support. Explain which part failed, whether you're the owner or invitee, and the device and app version on each side. Don't send passwords or verification codes. Review screenshots for personal information before attaching them.

Use the shared plan for the next shop

Once both people can find the right content, agree who will check the cupboards, who will add household extras and who is doing the shopping. A shared list still benefits from a clear handover.

Before leaving, compare the list with the plan and remove the things you already have. During the shop, use the same agreed list. Afterward, mention anything you couldn't buy so the person cooking can adjust the meal.

If you're setting up meals for the first time, follow the first meal plan and shopping list tutorial. For the broader device choice and household routine, see sharing a shopping list between iPhone and Android.
